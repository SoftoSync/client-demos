Business understanding

Retro HVAC serves heating, cooling and plumbing needs, including AC, furnaces, heat pumps and water heaters. The website already provides visible call/request-service actions, service information, FAQs and a contact form that collects name, email, phone, address and message. Those are useful foundations for a structured intake flow.

First step: confirm the public phone number and emergency-hours policy before configuring any call flow.

Recommended solution

Configure voice or web intake to capture service type, caller name, callback number, address, issue summary and preferred callback. Route by Retro-approved hours and escalation rules, then send a structured summary to the agreed inbox, CRM or field-service system after confirming which tools are already in use.

Safe routing

Use only approved urgency criteria and escalation contacts. Offer human takeover when the caller asks or the request is unclear.

Clear handoff

Send the team a concise request summary and track captured requests, transfers, incomplete intakes and staff corrections.

Guardrails

  • No technical diagnosis, unapproved safety advice, invented availability or automatic dispatch.
  • Use approved acknowledgements; confirm call-recording notices and SMS consent before enabling those features.

Phased delivery plan

Week 1 · Discover

Confirm phone, hours, emergency coverage, service area and current systems.

Week 2 · Design

Approve intake script, routing rules, privacy controls and human handoff.

Week 3 · Build & test

Connect the agreed channel and test urgent, after-hours and incomplete requests.

Week 4 · Pilot

Pilot one category, review summaries with staff and tune weak branches.

Indicative timeline: 3–4 weeks after discovery, access and scope agreement. Integration effort depends on the existing phone, CRM and scheduling systems.
